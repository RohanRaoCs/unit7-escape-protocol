Simulator

UNIT-7 has to collect three keycards, crack the vault lock at the terminal, and reach the exit. The facility fights back: a drone hunts it, laser gates switch on and off, coolant spreads, ceilings collapse onto its route and power surges rewire the lock. Every decision the agent makes is shown live on the right. All of it is Python code (the same package as the desktop app and the tests) running in your browser.

What these numbers mean
  • Path cost: every step costs 1, or 6 on coolant.
  • States explored: how many search nodes all of the agent's plans have expanded so far. Lower is more efficient.
  • Replans: how often the agent threw its plan away because the world changed.
  • Waits: ticks spent standing still, usually timing a laser gate.

The behaviour tree picks what to do next. It is checked from the top every tick, so a higher branch, like Survive, interrupts anything below it.

How to read this

Each curve is a fuzzy set, such as "Near". The dashed line marks the current value, and the number by each set name is how strongly the value belongs to it (0 to 1). The rules below fire with the strength of their weakest condition; their outputs are combined into the shaded shape, and its centre of mass is the threat score.

The vault code is a constraint satisfaction puzzle: five different digits that must satisfy every clue. During cracking the dials replay the solver's real search, including dead ends.
